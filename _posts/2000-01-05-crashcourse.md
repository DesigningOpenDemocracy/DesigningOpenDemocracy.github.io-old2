## Voting Methods

Most elections hand the whole decision to whoever gets the most votes. Here are a few alternatives that try to capture what voters actually think, or to treat minorities more fairly.

### Majority Judgment

Instead of picking one option, every voter gives each proposal a grade (e.g. *Excellent*, *Good*, *Passable*, *Poor*, *Reject*). The proposal with the best **median grade** wins. You never "waste" your vote, and there's no reason to vote tactically for a lesser evil.

* [MieuxVoter](https://mieuxvoter.fr/) - a French association promoting Majority Judgment
* [Majority Judgment Offline Urn](https://github.com/MieuxVoter/majority-judgment-offline-urn-android) - a libre Android app for running a vote on a single shared phone, with no internet needed ([F-Droid](https://f-droid.org/packages/com.illiouchine.jm))
* [Online version](https://app.mieuxvoter.fr/)

### Weighted Lottery

Majority-rule methods are *utilitarian*: they choose the greatest good for the greatest number, so a group that is always in the minority always loses. A **weighted lottery** draws the winner at random, with each option's chance equal to its share of support. If 7 people want burgers and 3 want sushi, burgers win 70% of the time and sushi wins 30% of the time. The majority still has the advantage, but the minority still has a real say. Over many repeated decisions, each group gets roughly its fair share.

A close relative is the **random ballot**: draw one ballot at random and follow it. It is the main voting rule under which voting honestly is always your best strategy (Gibbard, 1977).

The catch is that for one-off, high-stakes decisions, a lottery can produce an outcome most people would call clearly wrong. It suits everyday, repeated group choices better than life-or-death ones.
